# Plan to Succeed as WSP Senior Director — Government Affairs, Midwest

> Role read: A regional **Government Affairs / Government Relations** leader for WSP USA's
> Midwest geography. You are the firm's eyes, ears, and voice with government — building
> relationships with elected and appointed officials, shaping and tracking policy and
> infrastructure funding, managing political/regulatory risk, and translating all of it
> into pipeline and wins for the operating business lines. You influence revenue; you
> don't directly own a delivery P&L. Adjust to your exact states/metros and sectors
> (Transportation & Infrastructure, Water, Earth & Environment, Power & Energy, Buildings).

---

## 1. Define what success means (week 1)

Government Affairs is judged on *influence translated to business outcomes*. Lock 3–5
measurable outcomes with your manager (VP Government Affairs / regional executive):

- **Access & relationships:** Tier-1 relationships built/maintained with key officials,
  agency leaders (DOTs, water/utility commissions, MPOs), and legislative staff.
- **Policy outcomes:** Favorable legislative/regulatory positions advanced; threats
  neutralized; funding programs shaped or unlocked.
- **Funding intelligence → pipeline:** Early identification of IIJA/IRA, state, and local
  funding that converts into qualified pursuits for the business lines.
- **Business impact:** Influenced/sourced wins, agency relationships that produced RFQs,
  positioning on programs before they hit the street.
- **Reputation & presence:** WSP visible and credible in Midwest infrastructure policy
  circles; thought leadership; coalition leadership.
- **Compliance & risk:** Zero lobbying/ethics violations; clean registrations; political
  risk surfaced early.

**Action:** Write these as your scorecard. Government Affairs ROI is fuzzy unless you
define it up front — make the link to revenue explicit so the function stays funded.

## 2. First 90 Days

### Days 1–30 — Map the landscape and the internal demand
- **Manager alignment:** Mandate, budget, lobbyist/consultant retainers, PAC scope,
  decision rights, and the scorecard. Ask: "12 months out, what does great look like,
  and what would make this a failure?"
- **Internal stakeholders:** Meet the Midwest geography leaders and National Business Line
  leaders. Two questions: *Which agencies/programs matter most to you, and where do you
  need a door opened?* GA exists to serve their growth — know their targets.
- **Relationship audit:** Inventory existing relationships across each Midwest state —
  governors' offices, DOT secretaries, legislative transportation/infrastructure/
  appropriations committees, state agencies, big-city mayors/public works, MPOs, ports,
  transit/water authorities, federal delegation (House/Senate offices, regional EPA/DOT).
  Color-code: strong / thin / none.
- **Compliance check:** Review lobbying registrations in every state you'll touch, gift/
  ethics rules, pay-to-play restrictions, and PAC compliance. Get clean immediately.
- **External consultants:** Meet any contract lobbyists/firms WSP retains regionally;
  assess value and gaps.

### Days 31–60 — Build the plan and the funding map
- **Funding/policy calendar:** Build a Midwest map of money and rulemaking — IIJA/IRA
  formula + discretionary grants, state transportation/capital budgets and reauthorizations,
  water/PFAS and environmental rules, energy/grid programs, bonding measures, legislative
  sessions and key bill deadlines.
- **Priority issue list:** Pick the handful of policy issues where WSP has the most at
  stake and can credibly influence. Don't boil the ocean.
- **Quick wins:** A meeting that unlocks a pursuit, a coalition seat, an agency briefing,
  a comment letter on a pending rule — credibility builders.
- **Coalition/association strategy:** ACEC (national + state chapters), ASCE, WTS, AGC,
  state transportation/infrastructure coalitions, water associations — decide where WSP
  leads vs. participates.

### Days 61–90 — Commit and operationalize
- Publish a **one-page Midwest GA strategy:** priority states, priority agencies/officials,
  priority policy issues, the funding-to-pipeline engine, and your cadence.
- Stand up the **operating rhythm** (see §5) and the **funding-intel → BD handoff process**
  (see §3B) so insight reliably reaches the people who pursue work.
- Present the 12-month plan + scorecard to your manager and to the geography leadership.

## 3. The pillars to drive

### A. Relationships & access (the core asset)
- Systematically build relationships with **elected officials, agency executives, and —
  critically — career staff** (staff outlast politicians and write the rules).
- Be a **resource, not a supplicant:** bring technical expertise, data, and solutions to
  agencies' problems. WSP's value is credibility on infrastructure, not just asks.
- Maintain a **relationship CRM**: who, last touch, next touch, owner, what they care about.
- Show up between elections and outside of asks — trust is built in the quiet periods.

### B. Funding intelligence → pipeline (your highest-ROI output)
This is what makes GA pay for itself. Build a repeatable engine:
1. **Detect** funding/programs early (often 6–18 months before an RFQ).
2. **Qualify** fit against business-line capabilities.
3. **Hand off** to the right BD/capture lead with a warm agency relationship.
4. **Position** WSP with the agency *before* procurement starts (briefings, shaping scope,
   demonstrating expertise — within ethics rules).
5. **Track** conversion so you can prove GA influenced the win.

### C. Policy & advocacy
- Advance WSP-favorable positions on **funding levels, delivery methods (design-build, P3/
  progressive DB), procurement reform, and regulatory issues** (environmental permitting,
  PFAS/water, resilience, energy transition).
- Use **comment letters, testimony, coalition action, and direct advocacy.** Pick battles
  where WSP can move the needle and the stake is real.
- Be a **trusted technical advisor** to policymakers — the firm's deep bench is your
  credibility currency.

### D. Reputation, visibility & thought leadership
- Put WSP and its experts on **panels, committees, boards, and bylined pieces** on Midwest
  infrastructure priorities.
- Lead or co-lead a coalition or association role — leadership = influence + intel.
- Manage WSP's profile with agencies and media on regional infrastructure issues.

### E. Compliance, ethics & political risk
- **Non-negotiable:** flawless lobbying registration/reporting, gift/ethics compliance,
  pay-to-play and PAC compliance across every jurisdiction.
- Surface **political risk early** (leadership changes, budget shortfalls, hostile
  legislation, procurement controversies) to protect the business.
- Keep advocacy **bipartisan and issue-based** — infrastructure is durable across the
  aisle; relationships must survive elections.

## 4. Working the WSP matrix (the real unlock)

Government Affairs has **influence without authority** over the people who actually win and
deliver work. Your success depends on the operating units pulling you in.

- **Be demonstrably useful to business-line and geography leaders** — open doors, deliver
  intel, shorten their path to agencies. Make them advocates for the GA function.
- **Tight loop with BD/marketing and capture teams** — your intelligence is worthless if
  it doesn't reach the pursuit. Build the handoff and keep it warm.
- **Coordinate with corporate/national GA** so state, regional, and federal advocacy
  reinforce rather than collide.
- **Share credit generously.** When a relationship you built produces a win, let the
  pursuit team shine — it keeps them inviting you in.
- **Escalate cleanly** when geography and national priorities conflict; frame the tradeoff
  with a recommendation.

## 6. Midwest-specific landscape to master

- **States:** IL, IN, OH, MI, MN, WI, MO, IA (plus your assigned footprint) — each with
  its own DOT, capital program, legislative calendar, water/utility regulators, and
  political dynamics. Know the differences.
- **Money flows:** IIJA/IRA formula + discretionary (Bridge, Mega, RAISE, INFRA, water/
  PFAS, grid/energy), state DOT programs and gas-tax/bonding measures, transit and water
  authority capital plans.
- **Hot issues:** bridge/road condition and bridge programs, water/PFAS and lead-line
  replacement, transit funding, freight/rail, EV and grid, climate resilience, alternative
  delivery adoption, permitting reform.
- **Key agencies/bodies:** state DOTs, EPAs, water/PUC commissions, big-city public works,
  MPOs (CMAP-Chicago, SEMCOG-Detroit, Met Council-Twin Cities, MORPC-Columbus, etc.),
  ports/aviation authorities, transit agencies.

## 7. Common failure modes to avoid

- **GA as a cost center with no measurable link to revenue** — always connect activity to
  pipeline and wins, or the function loses funding and influence.
- **Only showing up to ask for things** — be a year-round resource, not a transactional one.
- **Compliance sloppiness** — one lobbying/ethics violation can damage the firm and your
  credibility instantly.
- **Partisanship** — relationships must survive the next election; stay issue-based.
- **Hoarding intel** — value is realized only when it reaches the pursuit team in time.
- **Ignoring career staff** — politicians come and go; staff write the rules and the RFQs.
- **Boiling the ocean** — too many issues/relationships, none deep. Prioritize ruthlessly.
