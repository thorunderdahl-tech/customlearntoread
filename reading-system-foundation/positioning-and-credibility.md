# Positioning & Credibility Guide

**How to talk about CustomLearnToRead so it holds up to teachers and researchers**
_Last updated: 2026-07-05_

The goal: when a reading specialist, a K–2 teacher, or a literacy researcher stumbles onto the
site, they should think _"these people actually know the science"_ — not _"another
personalization gimmick."_ Credibility with this audience is earned in a very specific way:
**precise vocabulary, honest claims, and transparency.** The fastest way to lose them is to
overclaim, which is exactly the trap that landed Fountas & Pinnell / Heinemann in a 2024
class-action over "research-backed" marketing. We stay on the right side of that line.

---

## 1. The one-liner

> **Personalized decodable readers built on a structured, systematic phonics
> scope-and-sequence — so children practice reading the exact patterns they're learning, in
> stories about themselves.**

That sentence signals three things an expert listens for: _decodable_, _systematic phonics_,
_scope-and-sequence_. It leads with the pedagogy, not the personalization.

---

## 2. Anchor to the frameworks, not to products

Cite the field's foundational, non-proprietary science. These are the names researchers respect
and that no company owns:

- **The Simple View of Reading** (Gough & Tunmer) — reading = decoding × language
  comprehension. Our decodable text develops the decoding half deliberately.
- **Scarborough's Reading Rope** — word recognition strands (phonological awareness, decoding,
  sight recognition) woven with language comprehension. Our scope-and-sequence maps to the
  word-recognition strands.
- **Ehri's phases of word reading / orthographic mapping** — why decoding practice (not
  guessing) is how words become automatic "sight words." This is the research reason our
  illustrations never invite word-guessing.
- **Structured Literacy** (International Dyslexia Association) — explicit, systematic,
  cumulative, diagnostic. Our engine is explicitly built this way.

Referencing these frameworks positions us _inside_ the science-of-reading consensus without
claiming we invented or proved anything.

---

## 3. Vocabulary: use these, avoid those

**Use (signals you know the field):**

- structured literacy · systematic, explicit phonics · scope-and-sequence · decodable text ·
  grapheme–phoneme correspondence · high-frequency words / "heart words" · orthographic mapping
  · cumulative review · controlled vocabulary · decodability

**Avoid (signals the opposite):**

- **"three-cueing," "MSV," "use the picture to figure out the word"** — discredited; a red flag
  to any informed teacher.
- **"sight words" used to mean whole-word memorization** — say _high-frequency words_ or
  _heart words_ and explain that most are taught by decoding the regular part.
- **"proven," "guaranteed," "research-proven results," "will make your child read"** — these
  are the overclaiming phrases that draw legal and expert fire. See §5.
- **"leveled readers"** without qualification — to this audience it evokes the F&P/guessing
  tradition. Say _decodable readers aligned to a scope-and-sequence._

---

## 4. How to describe the leveling (honestly)

> "Our ten reading levels are defined by an explicit phonics scope-and-sequence and a tracked
> set of high-frequency words. Each level is cross-walked to an approximate grade band so
> parents and teachers can place a child quickly."

- **Do** publish the scope-and-sequence and the skills matrix. Transparency _is_ the
  credibility.
- **Don't** brand the levels as Fountas & Pinnell (three-cueing lineage + active litigation) or
  claim official **Lexile** measures (proprietary to MetaMetrics; requires a signed license).
  You may say _"roughly equivalent to a [grade] reading level"_ — not _"Lexile 300L certified."_

---

## 5. Claims discipline (the F&P lesson)

A simple internal rule: **describe the method, not the outcome.**

| Say this (method, verifiable) | Not this (outcome, unprovable) |
|---|---|
| "Built on systematic, explicit phonics." | "Proven to make kids read." |
| "Text is decodable at each level." | "Guaranteed reading results." |
| "Aligned with structured-literacy principles." | "Research-proven / research-backed results." |
| "Practice for the patterns a child is learning." | "The science-based way every child learns." |

If we ever want to make an _outcome_ claim, it must be backed by our own collected evidence
(e.g., a pilot study) and stated with its limits. Until then, method claims only.

---

## 6. Audience-specific pitches

**To a teacher (30 seconds):**
> "Every book is decodable — kids sound out words using patterns they've already been taught,
> not guessing from pictures. It follows an explicit scope-and-sequence, and the stories are
> personalized so the practice actually feels worth doing. Here's the exact skills matrix behind
> each level."

**To a researcher (30 seconds):**
> "We separated the educational engine from the story content. The engine is a systematic
> phonics scope-and-sequence with tracked high-frequency words; a validator enforces
> decodability, controlled new-word introduction, and cumulative review on every generated
> book. Personalization adds motivating proper nouns without raising the decodable load. We
> publish the scope-and-sequence and validation rules — happy to have them critiqued."

**To a parent (30 seconds):**
> "Books your child can actually read — starring your child. As they learn new letter sounds,
> the stories use exactly those sounds, so reading feels like a win every time."

The parent version is warmer; the teacher/researcher versions lead with method and _invite
scrutiny_. Inviting scrutiny is itself a credibility signal.

---

## 7. Make transparency a feature (the trust moat)

The single strongest credibility move: **publish the methodology.** Put a page on the site —
"Our Approach / The Reading Science" — that includes:

1. The scope-and-sequence (levels + phonics + heart words) — straight from `reading-levels.json`.
2. The skills matrix — what each level teaches and expects.
3. The word-eligibility rule and the validator's checks (in plain language).
4. The frameworks we align to (§2), cited honestly.
5. A clear statement of what we _don't_ claim (§5).

Companies that hide their leveling look like marketing. Companies that publish it look like
educators. We want to be the second kind.

---

## 8. A credibility roadmap (cheap → strong)

1. **Publish the methodology page** (above) — free, immediate, high-trust.
2. **Recruit a named reading specialist / literacy-Ed.D. advisor** to review the
   scope-and-sequence and let us cite the review. One credible name changes the perception.
3. **Run a small pilot** (a classroom or reading tutor) and collect qualitative feedback — the
   start of real, ownable evidence.
4. **Only later, and carefully, make outcome claims** — with data and stated limits.

---

## 9. Skeptic FAQ (answers a teacher will test us with)

- **"Is this decodable or just leveled?"** → Decodable. Words are restricted to phonics patterns
  taught by that level, plus tracked high-frequency words. Here's the sequence.
- **"Do the pictures give away the words?"** → No — by design. Illustrations support meaning and
  motivation; children decode the text. We treat picture-guessing as a bug the validator guards
  against.
- **"How do you handle a child's name that isn't decodable?"** → It's treated as a personal
  proper-noun the child recognizes early and sees repeatedly, and we keep the rest of that page
  fully at-level so the decodable load doesn't rise.
- **"What's your evidence it works?"** → We don't claim proven outcomes. We build on
  structured-literacy principles and publish our method so you can judge it. We're running pilots
  to gather our own evidence.
- **"Is this Fountas & Pinnell leveling?"** → No. Our levels are defined by an explicit phonics
  scope-and-sequence, not by the three-cueing tradition.
